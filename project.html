About the Project

The Manhattan Project is often remembered through a few iconic locations: Los Alamos, Oak Ridge, and Hanford. However, the development of the American nuclear complex extended far beyond these sites.This project traces the geographic expansion of the American nuclear complex from World War II through the Cold War. By mapping the facilities, industries, and universities connected to this expansion, the project reveals how the United States transformed a secret wartime project into a nationwide industrial and military infrastructure and how the consequences of that transformation extended beyond the locations most commonly associated with the Manhattan Project.

A history with a geography

On the map, users can click on individual markers to learn about each location, its role in the development of the nuclear complex, and its connections to other sites. The map also highlights the local communities, workers, and landscapes affected by this transformation.

A starting point, not the whole story

The locations shown here are a selective introduction, not a complete inventory of the nuclear complex. The project looks beyond wartime mobilization toward the Cold War expansion that followed; new places and perspectives can continue to deepen that picture.
